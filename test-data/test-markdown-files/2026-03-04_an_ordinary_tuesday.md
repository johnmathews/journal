# 4 March 2026

*Grimmauld Place — late, everyone asleep*

Ginny says I scowl in my sleep now. I told her that's rich coming from someone who elbows me in the ribs every time Gwenog Jones comes on the wireless. She threw a pillow at me and said I deserve the scowling if I'm going to keep bringing work home in my jaw.

She's not wrong.

Today Robards called me into his office before I'd even got my coat off. Dawlish — *Dawlish*, of all people — has filed a formal complaint against me. Says I've been "undermining command structure" on the Carrow case. What he means is that I went round him to talk to the goblins at Gringotts directly, because he'd been sitting on the intelligence for eleven days and two more vaults had been hit in the meantime. What was I supposed to do, wait for him to finish his tea?

Robards knows. He *knows*. But there's a review board now, and apparently the Wizengamot has been getting twitchy about "Auror overreach" — which is a very funny phrase to hear applied to me, considering half the reason the Aurors still exist in their current form is because I rebuilt the department after the war. Not that anyone wants to say that out loud anymore. Ron says it's the new political weather. Everyone's decided twenty-seven years is long enough to be grateful for anything.

I keep thinking about what Hermione said at dinner on Sunday — that the Ministry is always most dangerous when it's bored. When there's no war, no crisis, it eats its own. She's running the DMLE now so I suppose she'd know, but it was still uncomfortable to hear her say it while Lily was asking for more pudding.

Lily turned twelve last month. Twelve. I still can't get used to it.

James owled from school — got himself into some row with a Slytherin over Quidditch and lost Gryffindor fifteen points. He wrote the letter like he was sending a battlefield report. I had to put it down and walk around the kitchen to stop laughing. Ginny is pretending to be cross with him but she's keeping the letter in her bedside drawer so I know she thinks it's brilliant too.

Albus didn't write. Albus never writes. He and Scorpius are probably plotting something in the library — Ginny says if we haven't heard from him it means he's happy, and I'm trying to believe her.

Tomorrow I've got to sit through the preliminary hearing with Dawlish and pretend I don't know he's being fed lines by someone higher up. I have a guess about who. I don't want to write it down even here.

Bed. Ginny's left the lamp on for me.

— H
