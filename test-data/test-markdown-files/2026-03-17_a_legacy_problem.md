# 17 March 2026

*Office, between meetings, half a sandwich*

I shouldn't be writing this here. If anyone walked in — but everyone's at the quarterly briefing and I've got twenty minutes and if I don't get some of this out of my head I'm going to hex the next person who speaks to me, which would rather prove Dawlish's point for him.

It's not Dawlish.

It was never really Dawlish. He hasn't got the imagination. I found out yesterday — through Ron, who heard it from someone in Magical Transport of all places, which is how you know the whole Ministry is already talking — that the complaint was *drafted* in the Minister's own office. Shacklebolt's been gone four years and I still keep expecting to walk past his portrait and have him wink at me, but this new lot — Thicknesse's great-niece runs the Minister's private office now, can you credit it, and she has apparently decided that I am a "legacy problem."

A legacy problem.

I wrote that phrase in my notebook and looked at it for about a full minute.

The thing is, I can see what she means. I *can*. I'm forty-five, I've been in the department for more than twenty years, and every time I walk into a room people still look at my forehead before my face. That's not a working condition, that's a haunting. Hermione's been telling me for three years I should move — advisory role, teaching position at the Academy, something. And I keep saying no because I genuinely love the work. I love walking into a crime scene and seeing the shape of it. I love the moment when a case breaks open. I don't love the politics but nobody loves the politics.

But now I'm wondering if she's right and I've just been too stubborn to see it. Not because I'm no good at the job — I'm better at it than I've ever been — but because being *me*, in *this* job, gives them something to push against. And I'm making it easier for them every time I go round Dawlish instead of just letting the case rot.

Ginny and I talked about it last night. Properly talked, not the kind of talking we do when we're both half asleep. She made tea and we sat at the kitchen table until one in the morning. She said — and this is the bit that's stuck — she said, "Harry, you don't have to keep proving you deserve to still be here. You're allowed to want something different."

I didn't know what to say to that. I still don't.

Lily's friend Rose Weasley came over at the weekend and the two of them were writing a play together about — I'm not entirely sure what, some sort of ghost murder mystery — and I watched them from the doorway for a bit and thought, I want to be in this house more. I want to be at the table when they're writing plays about ghosts. James finishes Hogwarts in two years. Albus in four. Lily in six. And I keep saying "when the Carrow case is over" and then there's another case.

Back to the briefing. Dawlish will be there. I'll be civil. I'll be *boringly* civil. Let her write a memo about that.

— H
